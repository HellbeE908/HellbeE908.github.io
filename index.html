SPECTER: a 3D LiDAR scanner for the price of a laptop

Terrestrial laser scanners cost ₹27–81 lakh before import duty, so most civic structures in India are still recorded with tapes and photographs. SPECTER rotates a 2D LiDAR to scan buildings, rooms and parks in 3D, and the complete unit costs ₹41,256, 60 to 190 times less.

I built SPECTER with one teammate, as our entry to the Vishwakarma Awards, and we are writing it up as an IEEE-format research paper.

An RPLIDAR C1 spins a laser in one plane. SPECTER tilts that plane through ±100° on a belt-driven spindle, so the slices stack into a full 3D point cloud. A Raspberry Pi 5 reads the sensor through a driver written from its protocol specification and exports LAS files that survey software opens directly. A XIAO ESP32-S3 generates the stepper pulses, because Linux can pause a process for milliseconds and every lost pulse would become a silent error in position.

Two findings came from testing against predictions rather than trusting the output. The first firmware moved the axis step by step and drifted 3.6° per motor revolution while reporting the requested angle; computing every move from the absolute target holds the error within ±0.11°. And mounting the sensor 50 mm off the axis did not even out the sampling as we expected: it spread the dense patch into a ring and left an unsampled hole 10 cm across. What the offset does do is keep the motor, bearings and electronics out of the scan plane.

On the merged park survey, planes fitted to the ground leave a median residual of 15.7 mm. Validation against a tape and a total station is in progress.

ARRSY: a crash detector that calls for help on its own

India recorded 4,61,312 road accidents and 1,68,491 deaths in 2022, according to the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, and help often arrives late. ARRSY, the Auto Rescue Response System, rides in the vehicle, recognises a crash or a rollover, and sends the location without anyone reaching for a phone.

The prototype is built around an Arduino Nano ESP32 with an MPU6050 motion sensor, a GPS receiver and a cellular modem. A companion Android app shows live sensor data and the vehicle’s orientation, and carries the emergency alert with the GPS position.

The hard part was false alarms. To a single sensor, a pothole or hard braking looks a lot like an impact, so the firmware cross-checks acceleration, rotation and tilt, filters out road vibration, and only then decides.

How the response is designed to work

  1. ARRSY switches on with the ignition and starts watching vibration, tilt and motion.
  2. It detects a crash or rollover, or someone presses the SOS button.
  3. A 15-second countdown starts, so a false alarm can be cancelled.
  4. If nobody cancels, the alert goes out to emergency services with the vehicle’s location.

Arduino Nano ESP32, MPU6050, GPS, cellular modem, TinyML in C and C++, Android. Built with a teammate and selected for the MVP round of Microsoft Imagine Cup 2023.
